Amazon’s 2025 was a strong year on paper: revenue grew 12%, profits hit a record, and AWS is still the main profit engine. The big story is spending. Capital spending jumped about 65% to build AI data centers, and that pushed free cash flow down by roughly 70%.

Headline numbers (fiscal year ended Dec 31, 2025)
	2024	2025	Change
Net sales	$638.0B	$716.9B	+12%
Operating income	$68.6B	$80.0B	+17%
Operating margin	10.8%	11.2%	
Net income	$59.2B	$77.7B	+31%
Diluted EPS	$5.53	$7.17	+30%
Operating cash flow	$115.9B	$139.5B	+20%
Capex (net)	$77.7B	$128.3B	+65%

Segments
North America: $426.3B in sales (+10%) and $29.6B in operating income, a 6.9% margin. Growth came from more units sold, advertising and subscriptions.
International: $161.9B in sales (+13%, or +10% without currency effects) and $4.8B in operating income, a 2.9% margin. Margins are still thin.
AWS: $128.7B in sales (+20%) and $45.6B in operating income. That’s 18% of revenue but 57% of operating income. Its margin slipped from 37.0% to 35.4% because of heavy infrastructure spending.

By revenue line: Advertising grew fastest at +22% to $68.6B. Subscriptions grew 12% to $49.6B, third-party seller services 10% to $172.2B, and online stores 9% to $269.3B.

What drove the year
AI infrastructure spending. Capex of $128B went mostly to data centers supporting AWS, and Amazon expects it to rise again in 2026. Technology and infrastructure expense grew 23%, far faster than revenue.
Net income was boosted by the Anthropic investment. “Other income” swung from a $2.3B loss to a $15.2B gain. Most of the gain came from marking up Amazon’s preferred stock in Anthropic and from converting its Anthropic notes into stock. That’s an accounting gain, not cash, so operating income is the cleaner read on the business. (For transparency: Anthropic makes me.)
One-time charges reduced operating income:
$2.5B to settle the FTC lawsuit.
$2.7B in severance for planned job cuts.
Without them, operating income would have been about $85B.
Taxes: The 2025 federal tax law brought back 100% bonus depreciation and immediate expensing of R&D. That cut cash taxes from $12.3B to $8.3B, and Amazon expects a similar benefit in 2026.
Balance sheet and backlog
Cash and marketable securities: $123.0B.
Long-term debt: $65.6B, up from $52.6B. Amazon raised $15.7B in new long-term debt during the year.
Total assets: $818B, up from $625B. Equity is $411B.
AWS contract backlog: About $244B of contracted future revenue not yet recognized, with a weighted average remaining life of 4.1 years. This is a key forward indicator for AWS.
Investments: Equity and convertible-debt holdings total $69.1B, mostly Anthropic, plus Rivian.
Employees: About 1.58 million.
Q1 2026 guidance (given Feb 5, 2026)
Revenue: $173.5–178.5B, up 11–15%.
Operating income: $16.5–21.5B, compared with $18.4B a year earlier. The forecast includes about $1B of extra costs for Amazon Leo, its satellite internet project, plus spending on quick commerce and lower international prices.
Key risks and legal issues
Competition and AI disruption: AI shopping agents and search could reroute customers away from Amazon.
China exposure: Sellers based in China make up a significant share of third-party seller and advertising revenue, so tariffs and trade disputes are a real risk.
Supply: Memory-chip shortages are named specifically.
Antitrust: Ongoing cases from the FTC, state attorneys general and private plaintiffs in the US, Canada and UK. They seek billions in damages and, in some cases, structural remedies.
Other legal matters:
A €746M EU privacy (GDPR) fine, under appeal.
An Italian competition fine, reduced to €752M, under appeal.
A $525M patent verdict for Kove over AWS, under appeal.
Several new patent suits against AWS and Prime Video.

Bottom line: The core business is healthy. Ads and AWS are growing at about 20%, and retail margins are slowly improving. The open question is whether Amazon’s huge AI infrastructure build pays off, since for now it has wiped out most of the free cash flow.

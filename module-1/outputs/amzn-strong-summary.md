## What each segment does

Amazon reports three segments: *"three segments: North America, International, and AWS"* (Item 8, Note 10, p.67 (PDF 100)).

- **North America** sells consumer products (its own and third-party sellers'), advertising and subscription services through North America-focused online and physical stores, including export sales (Note 10, p.68 (PDF 101), *"North America-focused online and physical stores"*).
- **International** is the same retail, advertising and subscription model through internationally focused online stores (Note 10, p.68 (PDF 101), *"internationally-focused online stores"*).
- **AWS** sells compute, storage, database and other services globally to start-ups, enterprises, governments and academia (Note 10, p.68 (PDF 101), *"global sales of compute, storage, database"*). Most AWS operating costs sit in "Technology and infrastructure" (Item 7, p.25 (PDF 43), *"Costs to operate our AWS segment"*).

- **Working-capital lines, FY2025 vs FY2024 ($B):** inventories (3.0) vs (1.9); accounts receivable (7.3) vs (3.2); other assets (15.6) vs (14.5); accounts payable +11.2 vs +3.0; accrued expenses (5.0) vs (2.9); unearned revenue (0.2) vs +4.0.
- **D&A definition matters.** The cash-flow D&A line also includes capitalized content and operating-lease asset amortization. D&A on property and equipment alone was $41.9B vs $32.1B (Note 10, p.71 (PDF 105), *"Total depreciation and amortization expense, by segment"*). Using that figure would cut FY2025 UFCF by $23.9B.
- **ETR distortion.** The 19.6% ETR is applied to operating income, but it reflects $97.3B of pre-tax income, which includes the $15.2B non-operating Anthropic gain.

## Company-specific risks

**Risk factors (Item 1A: potential, not yet realized)**

- **Data-center capacity mismatch.** Misjudging demand can leave excess or insufficient data-center capacity, causing impairments and outages (p.11 (PDF 18), *"excess or insufficient fulfillment or data center capacity"*). This matters more now: AWS D&A rose from $13.3B to $21.5B, +61% (Note 10, p.71 (PDF 105)), and capex is expected to rise again in 2026.
- **China seller and supplier concentration.** China-based sellers drive significant portions of third-party seller services and advertising revenue, so tariffs or trade disputes hit high-margin revenue lines (p.8 (PDF 13), *"China-based sellers account for significant portions"*).
- **Gatekeeper and antitrust regulation of the core model.** Open investigations target the stores, the fulfillment network, Prime and AWS cloud practices (p.15 (PDF 26), *"certain aspects of AWS's offering of cloud services"*).

**Actual events (already happened or pending)**

- **FTC settlement:** $2.5B charged in Q3 2025 to North America (Note 1, p.41 (PDF 62), *"$2.5 billion of expense related to the settlement"*).
- **Italy competition fine:** a €1.13B fine, already paid, was cut to €752M in Sept 2025. Amazon appealed in Dec 2025 (Note 7, p.61 (PDF 92), *"reduced the fine to €752 million"*).
- **Kove patent verdict:** a jury awarded $525M plus $148M of interest over S3 and DynamoDB. Amazon has appealed (Note 7, p.60 (PDF 90), *"awarded Kove $525 million in damages"*).
- **Antitrust class actions:** pending in the US, UK and Canada, seeking "billions of dollars" (Note 7, p.60 (PDF 90), *"seek billions of dollars of alleged damages"*).

## MD&A highlight

- **FCF is management's stated north star, and AI spending is pressuring it.** MD&A says the focus is long-term FCF growth (Item 7, p.20 (PDF 34), *"long-term, sustainable growth in free cash flow"*). It also warns that tech and infrastructure spending for AI can hurt near-term FCF (Item 7, p.21 (PDF 36), *"can negatively impact short-term free cash flow"*). FY2025 FCF fell 70.7% to $11.2B, so the long case depends on that capex earning a return.

## Management outlook

- **Q1 2026 guidance (issued Feb 5, 2026):** net sales of $173.5–178.5B (+11–15% YoY), including about 180 bp of FX tailwind. Operating income of $16.5–21.5B vs $18.4B in Q1 2025 (Item 7, p.30 (PDF 51), *"between $173.5 billion and $178.5 billion"*).
- **Known Q1 2026 drags:** about $1B more Amazon Leo (satellite) cost, quick-commerce investment and sharper international prices. Guidance assumes no acquisitions, restructurings or legal settlements (p.30 (PDF 51), *"higher year-over-year Amazon Leo costs"*).
- **Capex:** technology infrastructure (mostly AWS) and fulfillment capacity are both expected to increase in 2026 (Item 7, p.23 (PDF 39), *"both of which we expect to increase in 2026"*). A 2026 dollar figure is not included.
- **AI:** management expects to keep adding AI investment, with macro effects continuing into Q1 2026 (Item 7, p.24 (PDF 41), *"additional investments in our artificial intelligence initiatives"*). Memory-chip supply is named as a guidance risk (p.30 (PDF 51), *"including for memory chips"*).
- **Tax:** the 2025 Tax Act cut cash taxes from $12.3B to $8.3B, and a similar effect is expected in 2026 (Item 7, p.23 (PDF 39), *"similar effect on our cash taxes in 2026"*). It also raised the book provision through a smaller foreign income (FDII) deduction (Note 9, p.64 (PDF 97), *"increased our income tax provision"*). A forward ETR is not included.
